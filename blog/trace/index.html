Trace

Pick an animal. Follow the dots. Colour it in. A tiny drawing app for kids, made for the iPad and Apple Pencil.

Try it at kids.sauravsood.com ↗

Two screens

Choose an animal, then draw. That’s the whole app. Tap a picture to see it bigger.

Kids love drawing on an iPad, but most drawing apps are built for grown-ups. Too many buttons, menus that open by accident, and a palm resting on the glass that leaves a smudge every time. I wanted something a child could pick up and use on their own: big pictures, big colours, no reading.

So Trace is just that. Eight animals, each shown as a dotted outline. Draw over the dots in any colour you like. When you’re done, tap the star.

How it helps

Play first. The practice sneaks in.

  • GripHolding a Pencil is holding a pencil. Same grip they’ll use for writing.
  • ControlFollowing dots means slowing down, steering and stopping on purpose.
  • ShapesCurves, loops and straight lines, the same strokes that later become letters.
  • ColoursEleven to name, pick and mix. “Can the lion be blue?” Yes.
  • CalmNo ads, no timers, no scores. Nothing to buy. Nothing to lose.

What’s inside

Small on purpose.

  • 8 animalsCat, dog, fish, elephant, lion, bird, turtle, butterfly. All drawn from scratch, so there are no licence worries.
  • PressurePress harder, the line gets thicker, like a real crayon.
  • Palm-proofOnce the Pencil touches the screen, hands are ignored for a minute.
  • 11 · 3Eleven colours and three crayon sizes, plus an eraser and undo.
  • SavedEach animal remembers its drawing on the iPad. Come back to it later.
  • ConfettiThe star button. Every finished animal deserves a party.

Clear needs two taps, so one stray tap can’t wipe out a masterpiece. And if you add it to the iPad’s home screen, it opens full screen like a real app, with no browser bars to poke at.

How it was built

One morning, with Claude as the engineer.

I asked Claude to look at what already exists on GitHub for drawing with a Pencil in the browser. The pick was perfect-freehand, a tiny open-source library that turns Pencil pressure into strokes that look hand-drawn. Bigger whiteboard toolkits were far more than a tracing page needs.

The rest is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step. The dotted animal sits under a see-through drawing layer, so scribbles never hide the lines. It’s hosted on Cloudflare at its own address, kids.sauravsood.com, because more kids’ apps will live there.

From the first question to the app going up took about an hour. I made the calls; Claude wrote the code, drew the animals and fixed the one deploy hiccup along the way.

More apps for small people are on the way.
