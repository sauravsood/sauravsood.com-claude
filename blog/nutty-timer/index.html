Nutty Timer

Butter Beings makes nut butter from two ingredients. Getting it right every time takes a clock, a log and a very loud beep.

Open nutty-timer.sauravsood.com ↗

One roast

Every batch of almonds goes through the same five stages before it’s stone-ground into butter.

  1. 6′Roast 160°C
  2. 2′Rest
  3. 5′Roast 155°C
  4. 2′Rest
  5. 5′Roast 155°C

Now do that several times a day, with your hands full of hot trays. Which stage is this batch on? Was that the third batch or the fourth? A kitchen timer counts down once and forgets everything.

So I asked for four simple things: a start and stop button, a clear view of which stage the batch is in, a count of the batches done, and timings I could change later. Nothing more.

What it does

Three tabs, propped up on a phone or tablet next to the roaster.

  • RoastStage name, temperature and a big countdown. A loud beep-beep when each stage ends.
  • Batch logWeight, roaster, and how long each stage really ran: finished, ended early or skipped.
  • TodayBatches roasted today, and the changeover time between them.
  • GrindA card per wet grinder, all running at once. First pour, last pour, salt, stop. Pauses don’t count.
  • AnalyticsTwo weeks of roasting, and every stage compared with the recipe.
  • AwakeThe screen never goes dark mid-roast.

Built from the floor

Almost every change started with something going wrong during a real roast.

  • Out of stepThe beep drifted from the clock on screen. Now they stay in sync.
  • Late beepA missed alarm played when the next stage started. A browser audio quirk, fixed.
  • SleepyThe screen kept dozing off. Keeping it awake got much sturdier.
  • Today, not everAn all-time batch count was useless on the floor. It became “batches today”.
  • Late tapSomeone hits a step a minute late. Now the time can be corrected.
  • Bad Wi-FiA grinder run that can’t save waits on the device and tries again.

How it was built

I’m not a full-time developer. I didn’t need to be.

  1. 3 OctA prototype in Lovable. A working roast timer within a few days.
  2. 8 OctClaude takes over the code. I describe the problem, often from the roasting room; Claude makes the change and I merge it.
  3. 17 OctStage-timing analytics. Are the roasts actually following the recipe?
  4. 28 OctOff Lovable, onto my own Supabase and Cloudflare. All 73 batches logged so far came along.
  5. NovIts own address: nutty-timer.sauravsood.com.

Ten pull requests between 8 October and 9 November, each one small, each one fixing a single real annoyance. Moving off the no-code tool brought the running cost down to almost nothing, and now I own the whole thing.

Under the hood it’s React and Tailwind, with Supabase for the roast and grinder logs, hosted on Cloudflare and deployed straight from GitHub. Tests cover the timing and analytics maths.

The best part isn’t the timer. It’s the log. When your product is just nuts and salt, consistency is everything, and now we can see whether every batch got the same roast.

Two ingredients. One very loud beep.
