Chores without words

Every task app is a list of words. What if the person doing the work can’t read? Meet Handmaiden.

Open tasks.sauravsood.com ↗

The plants were supposed to be watered on Monday and Thursday. The fridge gets cleaned on the last day of the month. The brass needs polishing before the festival. In most homes like ours, all of that lives in someone’s head, in a phone call, or in a voice note that scrolls out of sight by Wednesday.

The person running the house knows what needs doing and when. The person doing it is often a domestic worker who doesn’t read, or doesn’t read the household’s language. And almost every task app ever made is a list of words.

The gap

Before building anything, we looked at what already exists. Two halves, and nothing that joins them.

Household & to-do apps

Great schedules, several users, reminders.

All words.

Picture schedule apps

Made for children and non-readers. A picture and a tick really works.

One device. No one can assign from afar.

Handmaiden

A proper admin side for the household, and a worker side made only of pictures.

For the worker

Designed for someone most software leaves out. On a cheap Android phone.

  • No typingScan a QR code, see your own face, tap a four-picture code. A PIN made of pictures.
  • Big photosToday’s jobs are a grid of photos. Tap one for picture steps.
  • One tickDone? Tap the big tick. A photo as proof only when the task asks for it.
  • No clocksTime of day is shown as a picture, not as numbers.
  • Words optionalA small toggle shows the written task, so it can be shown to someone who reads.
  • OfflineInstalls to the home screen. Ticks are sent when the signal comes back.

For the household

The admin side can be as detailed as you like.

  • SchedulesOnce, or every few days, weeks, months or years. Chosen weekdays, “last day of the month”, or “10 days after it was last done”.
  • PhotosA cover photo, more pictures, picture steps, priority, labels and time of day.
  • One eachAssign a task to three people and each gets their own copy, so nobody’s tick hides someone else’s.
  • ReviewApprove, flag, comment with a photo, or send it back. Every attempt is kept, like an issue tracker’s history.
  • Day to dayToday board, overdue list, skip a day, leave, live updates. Several admins per home.
  • TrendsOn-time rates, streaks, workload and a gallery of proof photos, with CSV export.

How it was built

Idea to live app in about a week, with Claude as the engineer and me making the product calls.

  1. 25 OctResearch. Claude surveyed the existing apps and recommended a small custom build, borrowing a proven scheduling model instead of inventing one.
  2. 28 OctDecisions. Photo proof only when asked, one copy per worker, text behind a toggle, full review history.
  3. 30 OctBuild. Database, security rules, schedules, both apps, sign-in, analytics, offline support and tests, in one day.
  4. 31 OctLive at tasks.sauravsood.com.
  5. 2 NovFirst polish fix.

Under the hood it’s React and TypeScript on Cloudflare, with Supabase for the database, sign-in, photos and live updates. Each household only ever sees its own data. Repeating tasks are worked out inside the database on a timer, and a test throws hundreds of random schedules at the app and the database to make sure they agree.

What I liked most was the split. I decided what mattered for the people using it. Claude did the research, wrote the code and set up the hosting.

What’s next

Even fewer words.

  • LanguagesThe admin writes in their language; the worker reads in theirs. Translated automatically.
  • VoiceRecord a spoken instruction. It plays from a speaker button on the photo.

Software that respects the person doing the work.
